# IRC:SP:55-2014 — Guidelines on Traffic Management in Work Zones (First Revision)

**Code:** IRC:SP:55-2014
**Title:** Guidelines on Traffic Management in Work Zones
**Issuing Body:** Indian Roads Congress
**Status in DPR review:** Mandatory reference for any DPR that involves construction, maintenance, widening, utility laying, or repair on an in-service road where traffic is to be managed during execution.
**Companion codes:** IRC:67-2012 (Road Signs) for sign shapes, colours, legends and sheeting types; MoRTH Specifications for works execution.

> Extraction scope: mandatory requirements, design parameters, design tables, and formulas only. Theory, background narrative, derivations, and illustrative examples are excluded. Clause numbers are cited as `Cl. x.x` and refer to IRC:SP:55-2014.

---

## 1. SCOPE & APPLICABILITY

- These guidelines govern **all traffic control devices and traffic management arrangements** used on street/highway construction, maintenance, utility laying or repair. All such devices **shall conform** to these provisions. (Cl. 5)
- A **Work Zone Traffic Management Plan (WTMP)** is required wherever a work zone temporarily disrupts normal traffic flow. (Cl. 1.2, 3.4)
- Applies in both **rural and urban** situations and across all road categories including access-controlled highways (with stricter sheeting requirement — see §5.1).

---

## 2. WTMP — MANDATORY PLAN REQUIREMENTS (Cl. 3.4, 3.5, 3.6)

A compliant DPR **shall** demonstrate the following:

- A WTMP describing the traffic control measures for safe and efficient movement of road users through the work zone.
- WTMP **prepared by qualified/trained personnel** knowledgeable in safety principles, traffic control measures and the work activities; device design, selection and placement based on engineering judgment.
- Traffic-control planning **completed prior to occupying the work zone** for all construction, utility, and maintenance works (including minor works), and covering **all** categories of road users.
- **Urban works:** added attention to pedestrians and non-motorised users; works preferably at night; peak-hour repairs avoided; coordination of traffic control with construction activity.

---

## 3. TEMPORARY TRAFFIC CONTROL (TTC) ZONE — STRUCTURE (Section 4)

Every TTC zone **shall** be laid out in the following sequence (Cl. 4.2):

1. **Advance Warning Zone** — drivers warned and speed reduced via a series of signs over length **AWL**, with successive signs at minimum spacing **d**.
2. **Approach Transition Zone** — traffic redirected from normal path via the **Approach Taper (length ATL)** or circular curves of radius **SR**.
3. **Activity Zone** — comprises **Work Space + Traffic Space + Buffer Space** (Approach Longitudinal Buffer ALB, Exit Longitudinal Buffer ELB, Lateral Buffer Space LBS). No work activity or material storage permitted within any buffer space. (Cl. 4.2.3)
4. **Terminal Transition Zone** — traffic returned to normal path via the **Terminal Taper (length TTL)** or circular curves.
5. **Work Zone End** — extends from end of terminal taper to the last traffic-control device (end-of-roadwork / speed-limit sign). (Cl. 4.2.5)

### 3.1 Design formulas (Fig. 4.1)

| Quantity | Formula | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Approach Taper Length | **ATL = S1 × N_a** | S1 = lateral shift on approach side (m); taper rate = 1:N_a |
| Terminal Taper Length | **TTL = S2 × N_t** | S2 = lateral shift on terminal side (m); taper rate = 1:N_t |
| Curve option | radius = **SR** | Used instead of straight taper; SR values per Table 4.1; also applies to median crossovers |

Terminal taper rate is flatter (lower 1:N) than approach because plying traffic is already at reduced speed.

## 5. TRAFFIC CONTROL DEVICES (Section 5)

### 5.1 Road signs

- **Retro-reflective sheeting (mandatory minimum):** **Class B** (High Intensity Prismatic, ASTM D4956-09 Type III & IV per IRC:67-2012) for normal work zones; **Class C** (Micro Prismatic, ASTM D4956-09 Type VIII & IX) for **access-controlled highways**. (Cl. 5.1.1)
- Sign design (font/legibility) **shall** be governed by the speed at which the sign is read. (Cl. 5.1.3)
- Work zone regulatory signs: white text/arrows/border on **red** background. Work zone warning signs: triangular (normal warning) or rectangular (work-zone-specific), **black on yellow**. Informatory/direction signs: black on yellow. Sign boards designed in English + Hindi/regional language. (Cl. 5.1)
- **Mounting:** signs on barricades/portable supports **≥ 0.3 m** above travelled way; large signs (> 5 m²) on multiple breakaway posts **≥ 2.1 m** above ground. (Cl. 5.1.2)

- **STOP sign (octagonal) height:** 750 / 900 / 1200 mm for approach speed ≤50 / 51–65 / >65 kmph. (Cl. 5.1.4.1)
- **GIVE WAY sign (triangular) side:** 600 / 900 / 1200 mm for approach speed ≤50 / 51–80 / >80 kmph. (Cl. 5.1.4.1)

### 5.2 Channelizing devices

#### Traffic cones (Cl. 5.2.1)
- Heights: 500 mm, 750 mm, 1000 mm; base 300–500 mm.
- Night use: **shall** be retro-reflectorised. Cones 750–1000 mm: 150 mm wide white band, 75–100 mm from top, plus a lower band. Cones >900 mm: two bands 100–150 mm wide; minimum **two red + two white** stripes.
- Min **300 mm** clear distance from traffic-lane edge marking to cones.
- **Not** to be used for pedestrian channelization/barriers.

**Table 5.9 — Cone spacing**
| Speed | Cone size | Spacing in transition area | Spacing in straight portion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Up to 65 kmph | 500 mm | 1.5 m | 9 m |
| 66–100 kmph | 750 mm | 1.5 m | 9 m |
| > 100 kmph | 1000 mm | 1.5 m | 9 m |

#### Tubular markers (Cl. 5.2.2)
- Flexible plastic bollards; min **50 mm** width of reflective band displayed to traffic; min **300 mm** clear from lane marking.

**Table 5.10 — Tubular marker spacing**
| Speed (kmph) | Marker/bollard size | Opposite streams | Same-direction streams | At pavement-edge drop |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Up to 65 | 450 mm | 9 m | 6 m | 9 m |
| 66–100 | 700 mm | 9 m | 6 m | 9 m |
| > 100 | N.A. | Not applicable | Not applicable | Not applicable |

#### Hazard markers (Cl. 5.2.3)
- Alternating **yellow/black** retro-reflective stripes at **45°** sloping down in direction of traffic; OHM (left/right) or Two-Way Hazard Marker (TWHM). Required on all exposed roadside fixed objects and at diversion/gore areas.

#### Drums (Cl. 5.2.4)
- Min height **800 mm**, min width **300 mm** regardless of orientation.
- Horizontal circumferential alternating **red/white** retro-reflective stripes 100–150 mm wide; min **two red + two white**, **top stripe red**; non-reflective gaps **≤ 75 mm**; closed tops; drain holes in freezing regions.
- **Steel drums shall not be used.** Spacing per Table 5.9. Not for pedestrian channelization unless continuous.

#### Barricades (Cl. 5.2.5)
- Type I & II = portable; Type III & IV = permanent. Dimensions per Table 5.11 (rail length ~2.0–2.5 m).
- Barricade/sign combinations **shall be crashworthy**.
- Where used to channelize pedestrians: continuous detectable bottom and top rails, no gaps; bottom of bottom rail **≤ 900 mm**... (detectable edging, Cl. 5.2.9).

### 5.3 Pavement markings & road studs (Cl. 5.3)
- Temporary markings and road studs as channelizing aids; conform to IRC:67-2012 colour scheme.

### 5.4 Lighting & Variable Message Signs (Cl. 5.4)
- Flashing warning beacons, warning lights, portable VMS, **arrow boards/panels** for merging/diverging guidance. Arrow panel colour **yellow**; finished in non-reflective black; dimming ≥50%; flashing rate 25–40 per minute; on-time ≥50%.

---

## 6. VULNERABLE ROAD USERS (Section 6)
- Continuous segregated, detectable barriers for pedestrians and cyclists (Cl. 6.3).
- Alternative footpath/way provided when footpath is occupied (Cl. 6.4).
- Speed-reduction measures provided on approaches (Cl. 6.5).

---

## 7. TRAFFIC MANAGEMENT PRACTICES AT WORKSITES (Section 7)
Permitted operating methods, to be selected per site conditions:
- Alternate one-way operation: give-and-take, priority signs, stop/go boards, or portable signals (Cl. 7.1).
- Detours, diversions, full/intermittent/lane closures, lane constrictions, median crossovers, shoulder-as-lane, night construction (Cl. 7.2–7.10).

---

## 8. ROLES & RESPONSIBILITIES (Section 8)
DPR/contract documents **shall** assign responsibilities among: road authority, road operator, project director/in-charge, designer, road safety auditors, contractor, concessionaire, supervision consultant/RE, local police, and community. (Cl. 8.2)

---

## 9. DEPLOYMENT OF PERSONNEL & SAFETY COMPLIANCE (Section 9)

### 9.1 Flagman (Cl. 9.2)
- Flagman **shall** be physically fit, trained, alert; provided with hand-signalling devices (flags, sign paddles).
- Flagman **must** wear warning garments, safety headgear, footwear and gloves; night garments **of reflective material**.
- Signalling **flags** min **600 mm × 600 mm**, good red cloth, on a rigid handle.
- Hand **paddles** octagonal/round **600 mm × 600 mm**, with red/yellow/green **Class B** retro-reflective sheeting (IRC:67-2012) for night use. STOP face = red octagon; SLOW = yellow with black letters; GO = green.
- Flagman station visible to drivers at adequate distance (reduced to **20–50 m** in constrained conditions).

### 9.2 Reflective clothing (Cl. 9.3)
- All workers, supervisors and inspecting officers **shall** wear high-visibility fluorescent clothing with retro-reflective material, conspicuous from **100 m**.
- **360° visibility** with at least one retro-reflective band encircling the torso; appropriate band separation on torso, sleeves, trousers.

**Table 9.1 — Safety jacket colour**
| User | Fluorescent jacket colour |
|---|---|
| Workers exposed to traffic | Red–Orange / Orange |
| Supervising engineer & visiting higher officials | Yellow |

### 9.3 Roll-up signs (Cl. 9.4)
- Portable, changeable; retro-reflective sheeting **Class B** (IRC:67-2012); mounted on portable stands with back-support ribs.

### 9.4 Protective gear (Cl. 9.5)
- Workers provided appropriate PPE (head, foot, hand protection) for the activity.
